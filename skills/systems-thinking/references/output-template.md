# Output Template

Use for **Standard** and **Full** tiers. For Standard, keep sections short and merge or drop ones with nothing substantive to say — an empty heading is worse than an absent one. For Full, include all of them.

Order matters: the reader should be able to stop after section 3 and still have the answer.

---

## 0. Recommendation up front

Two or three sentences: what to do, what structure it changes, and the one condition most likely to invalidate it. Everything below is the argument for this.

## 1. Decision question

The decision, stated so that a yes or no is meaningful.

## 2. System boundary

What is inside the model, what is outside, and what was deliberately moved inside after the boundary challenge.

## 3. Visible events

The observable symptoms that triggered attention.

## 4. Patterns

Behaviour over time — trends, cycles, recurrence. Note where time-series evidence is missing.

## 5. Hidden structure

The structures that generate those patterns.

## 6. Mental models

Assumptions holding the current structure in place, with the evidence for and against each.

## 7. Stocks and flows

```
Inflows → [ STOCK ] → Outflows
```

For each: direction of change, rate if known, and whether anyone is measuring it.

## 8. Actors and goals

Who acts, what state they want, what they control, what they know.

## 9. Goal conflicts

Where actors pull the system in incompatible directions.

## 10. Causal structure

Major relationships with sign, delay, confidence, and conditionality.

## 11. Feedback loops

Labelled R1, R2, B1, B2… Name which currently dominates and what would shift dominance.

## 12. Delays

Where time lags hide effects, and in which direction they mislead.

## 13. Nonlinearities

Thresholds, saturation, tipping points, interaction effects, hysteresis.

## 14. Policy resistance

How the system could weaken, absorb, or defeat the intervention — with the resistance loop drawn.

## 15. Effects

Direct, indirect, delayed, distributed, and recursive consequences. For each: who benefits, who absorbs the cost, which stock moves. Do not label any of these "side effects".

## 16. Uncertainties

Split explicitly:

- **Known** — established, evidenced.
- **Assumed** — believed, load-bearing, unverified.
- **Unknown** — not currently knowable with available information.

Flag the critical unknowns: the ones where being wrong changes the recommendation.

## 17. Competing hypotheses

At least three, each with the evidence that would confirm or eliminate it.

## 18. Leverage points

Ranked, with the leverage level named (low / medium / high) and feasibility noted.

## 19. Candidate interventions

Meaningful alternatives, including doing nothing where relevant. Include at least one option that is cheaper and more reversible than the recommendation.

## 20. Dynamic consequences

For each candidate, trace the chain:

```
Action
↓
Initial system response
↓
Actor adaptation
↓
Feedback
↓
Stock changes
↓
Delayed effects
↓
Possible nonlinear transition
↓
Long-term system state
```

## 21. Recommendation in full

- **Recommended intervention**
- **Why**
- **What system structure it changes**
- **Expected feedback response**
- **Expected policy resistance**
- **Key assumptions**
- **Major uncertainties**
- **Potential tipping points**
- **Signals to monitor** — including leading indicators of failure, not only of success. Specify what to watch, at what cadence, and what value would count as a warning.
- **Conditions requiring reconsideration** — the observations that should trigger revisiting this decision.

---

## Confidence statement

Close with an explicit confidence level and its basis. If the ten gate questions in SKILL.md cannot be answered, state which ones, lower confidence accordingly, and specify what would have to be learned — and how it could be learned cheaply.
